Timegrapher 1.1 measures a mechanical watch from the sound of its beats. It adds 45° positions and the Dynamic Poise Test, which shows which way a balance is out of poise, plus plain-text reports, a tooth and leaf calculator, and a long list of fixes from a full watchmaker-style shakedown. It states what it measured, with no grades or verdicts: reading the figures is up to you.

Download the file for your computer below. There is now a 64-bit ARM Linux download too, for a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 and other ARM boards. The steps for opening it the first time are in [Installing Timegrapher](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/blob/main/docs/reference/install.md). The builds are not signed, so macOS and Windows warn you once for each new version.

## What's new

- **45° positions:** set the Current Position card to **10** for the four stops between the crown positions, 1H30, 4H30, 7H30 and 10H30. A Positions Test gives each one its own row (#1473).
- **Dynamic Poise Test:** the rate in eight upright positions, 45° apart. It shows the wave the rate makes once round, its size and its fastest hour, and, where the amplitude allows, which way the balance's heavy spot hangs. It is in the app, the command line tool and the Claude Desktop extension (#1474).
- **Complication Test:** a chronograph or other complication off, then on, then off again, in one position, and what it changes in amplitude, rate and beat error (#1583).
- **Plain-text reports:** **Save as Text…** in the Positions Test and the Dynamic Poise Test writes a report to keep, print or send (#1508, #1509).
- **Wind on the Listen card:** set when the watch was last fully wound, beside Average Over. Each take is saved with its hours since full wind (#1915).
- **Tooth and leaf calculator:** type the teeth and leaves in **Manage Calibres** and it works out each wheel's period (#399). Steadiness marks each pinion's mesh beside the wheel lines (#1472).
- **Readings Over Time** reads out the time and the reading under the pointer (#1277).
- **BE Angle:** the beat error as an angle of the balance's swing, in the Beat Error card's hover (#1591).
- **Still Paper** on the Paper Strip: the paper stays put and the pen sweeps along it.
- **Run After Saving a Take:** a setting that runs a command of your own each time a take is saved. The command line tool's `excerpt` writes part of a recording as a plain FLAC file (#1478).
- **Problem reports** now carry the take's analysis, so the whole take can be read again (#400).
- **More calibres,** with the sources for each figure linked in the Calibres pane.

## What's better

- **Accuracy:** a slow watch is no longer misread after a long stretch of noise before its first beats (#1423). A scatter window fitted on only a few seconds of good beats is left out (#1432). Periods allow for a watch's ordinary slow wander, so its timing is no longer marked as a misfit (#1219). Live readings and the same take opened later read the beats the same way and on the same clock (#1327, #1342). Amplitude is kept when the lift angle changes (#1333), and the Tick Tock profile finds the unlock at a low amplitude (#1928).
- **Figures:** every figure is written to its fixed number of places, and each ± comes from at least 30 values (#1470). Where a ± is missing, the app says why (#1908). Positions figures no longer take a missing wind age as full wind (#1896).
- **Reliability:** a microphone that fails during a Power Reserve Test now ends the test with a message and keeps its take (#1882). Long live takes respond faster.
- **The shakedown:** a watchmaker-style shakedown of 1.1 found more than 40 problems, and they are fixed. The list is in #1998.

## Takes saved by 1.1 and 1.0

1.1 opens takes saved by 1.0. 1.0 opens most takes saved by 1.1, but not a Complication Test, whose `session.toml` notes each stretch (a `complication` line) that 1.0 doesn't know. Update the app, the command line tool and the extension together.

It is also [on GitHub](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/blob/main/docs/user-guide/README.md).

## What you need

- A contact microphone in a watch holder. An ordinary microphone often hears the loud drop but misses the quiet unlock, so rate works but amplitude doesn't.
- A mechanical watch with a Swiss lever escapement.
- Its calibre, if you know it. The calibre gives the lift angle, which amplitude needs.
- For the 45° positions and the Dynamic Poise Test, a stand that holds the watch upright at 45° steps.

## Telling us what you found

Use **Help > Report a Problem…** in the app to make a zip of what went wrong, or [open an issue](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/issues/new/choose). Write down what you were doing, what you expected and what happened. A screenshot or the saved take folder helps a lot.
